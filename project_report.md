## 1. Executive Summary

This report presents a data-driven analysis of **3,900 retail transactions** to uncover customer shopping patterns, product performance, discount effectiveness, and loyalty dynamics. Using SQL-based analytics on a cleaned and feature-engineered dataset, 10 business queries were designed and executed to derive actionable commercial insights.

**Key findings at a glance:**

- Total revenue across all transactions: **$233,081**
- Average transaction value: **$59.76** (range: $20–$100)
- **79.9%** of customers are classified as **Loyal** (>10 prior purchases)
- Discounts are applied on **43%** of transactions without eroding average spend
- **Young Adults** are the highest revenue-contributing demographic ($62,143)
- **Clothing** dominates at **$104,264** in revenue (44.7% of total)

## 2. Dataset Overview

### 2.1 Source Data

The raw dataset (`customer_shopping_behavior.csv`) contains 3,900 records across 18 columns capturing customer demographics, purchase details, product information, and behavioral metadata.

| Attribute | Details |
|---|---|
| **Records** | 3,900 transactions |
| **Customers** | 3,900 unique Customer IDs |
| **Products** | 25 distinct items across 4 categories |
| **Locations** | 50 U.S. states |
| **Age Range** | 18–70 years |
| **Purchase Range** | $20–$100 |

### 2.2 Data Quality & Cleaning

Prior to analysis, the following data quality measures were applied in `data_cleaning.ipynb`:

| Issue Identified | Records Affected | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Missing `Review Rating` values | 37 rows (0.95%) | Imputed using **median rating per product category** to preserve category-level distribution patterns |
| Inconsistent column naming | All 18 columns | Standardized to **snake_case** (e.g., `Purchase Amount (USD)` → `purchase_amount`) |
| Redundant column | `promo_code_used` | Verified **100% equivalence** with `discount_applied` using `.all()` check; dropped redundant column |

### 2.3 Feature Engineering

Two new features were engineered to enable richer segmentation:

| Feature | Method | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `age_group` | `pd.qcut(age, q=4)` → Young Adult, Adult, Middle-aged, Senior | Quantile-based demographic segmentation ensuring equal group sizes |
| `purchase_frequency_days` | Dictionary mapping (e.g., Weekly → 7, Monthly → 30) | Converted categorical frequency labels to numerical day values for quantitative analysis |

## 3. Analysis Findings

### 3.1 Revenue by Gender (Q1)

> *What is the total revenue generated by male vs. female customers?*

```sql
SELECT gender, SUM(purchase_amount) AS revenue
FROM customer
GROUP BY gender;
```

| Gender | Revenue ($) | Share (%) | Customer Count | Avg. Spend ($) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Male** | 157,890 | 67.7% | 2,652 | 59.54 |
| **Female** | 75,191 | 32.3% | 1,248 | 60.25 |
| **Total** | **233,081** | **100%** | **3,900** | **59.76** |

**Insight:** While males account for 67.7% of total revenue, this is driven by **sample composition** (68% of records are male) rather than spending behavior. The per-transaction average is nearly identical ($59.54 vs $60.25), with female customers spending marginally more per transaction.

**Recommendation:** The revenue gap is a volume gap, not a spend-intensity gap. Marketing efforts should focus on expanding female customer acquisition, as their per-transaction value is already competitive.

### 3.2 High-Value Discount Shoppers (Q2)

> *Which customers used a discount but still spent more than the average purchase amount?*

```sql
SELECT customer_id, purchase_amount 
FROM customer 
WHERE discount_applied = 'Yes' 
  AND purchase_amount >= (SELECT AVG(purchase_amount) FROM customer);
```

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Average purchase amount (threshold) | $59.76 |
| Customers who used discounts AND exceeded average | **839** |
| Total discount users | 1,677 |
| Proportion of discount users who are high-value | **50.0%** |

**Insight:** Half of all discount-using customers are spending **above the average** transaction value. These 839 customers represent "smart discount users" — they leverage promotions but do not trade down in purchase value.

**Recommendation:** This cohort is ideal for **loyalty program enrollment** and **premium product upselling**. They demonstrate price-awareness combined with high willingness to spend — a prime cross-sell profile.

### 3.5 Subscription Status: Revenue Analysis (Q5)

> *Do subscribed customers spend more than non-subscribers?*

```sql
SELECT subscription_status,
       COUNT(customer_id) AS total_customers,
       ROUND(AVG(purchase_amount), 2) AS avg_spend,
       ROUND(SUM(purchase_amount), 2) AS total_revenue
FROM customer
GROUP BY subscription_status;
```

| Status | Customers | Avg. Spend ($) | Total Revenue ($) | Revenue Share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Non-Subscribers** | 2,847 | 59.87 | 170,436 | 73.1% |
| **Subscribers** | 1,053 | 59.49 | 62,645 | 26.9% |

**Insight:** Subscribers and non-subscribers spend nearly the same per transaction ($59.49 vs $59.87 — a $0.38 difference). The subscription program is not generating measurably higher per-customer revenue. Only 27% of the customer base subscribes, and they do not out-spend non-subscribers.

**Recommendation:** The subscription value proposition needs restructuring. Consider tying subscription benefits to **exclusive discounts, early access to new products, or free shipping** to create a tangible spending differential. The current offering does not incentivize increased spend.

### 3.6 Discount Penetration by Product (Q6)

> *Which products have the highest discount application rates?*

```sql
SELECT item_purchased,
       ROUND(100.0 * SUM(CASE WHEN discount_applied = 'Yes' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)
             / COUNT(*), 2) AS discount_rate
FROM customer
GROUP BY item_purchased
ORDER BY discount_rate DESC
LIMIT 5;
```

| Rank | Product | Discount Rate (%) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hat | 50.00 |
| 2 | Sneakers | 49.66 |
| 3 | Coat | 49.07 |
| 4 | Sweater | 48.17 |
| 5 | Pants | 47.37 |

**Overall discount statistics:**

| Discount Applied | Transactions | Avg. Spend ($) |
|---|---|---|
| No | 2,223 | 60.13 |
| Yes | 1,677 | 59.28 |

**Insight:** The top 5 products by discount rate cluster tightly at 47–50%, suggesting discounts are uniformly distributed rather than concentrated on specific products. Critically, the average spend for discounted transactions ($59.28) is only **$0.85 lower** than non-discounted ones ($60.13) — discounts are not eroding basket value.

**Recommendation:** The discount strategy is healthy — it drives engagement without cannibalizing revenue. Maintain current discount levels on high-volume items (Hats, Sneakers, Coats). Consider reducing discounts on items where they don't influence purchase decisions to recapture margin.

### 3.7 Customer Loyalty Segmentation (Q7)

> *How does the customer base segment by purchase history?*

```sql
WITH customer_type AS (
    SELECT customer_id, previous_purchases,
           CASE 
               WHEN previous_purchases = 1 THEN 'New'
               WHEN previous_purchases BETWEEN 2 AND 10 THEN 'Returning'
               ELSE 'Loyal'
           END AS customer_segment
    FROM customer
)
SELECT customer_segment, COUNT(*) AS "Number of Customers"
FROM customer_type
GROUP BY customer_segment;
```

| Segment | Definition | Customers | Share (%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Loyal** | >10 previous purchases | 3,116 | 79.9% |
| **Returning** | 2–10 previous purchases | 701 | 18.0% |
| **New** | 1 previous purchase | 83 | 2.1% |

**Insight:** This is a **mature, retention-heavy customer base**. Nearly 80% of customers have made more than 10 prior purchases. The acquisition pipeline (New customers at 2.1%) is extremely narrow, which could be a risk factor if loyal customers churn.

- **Retention focus:** Invest in keeping the 3,116 loyal customers engaged — churn prevention programs, exclusive offers, and personalized recommendations.
- **Acquisition concern:** A 2.1% new customer rate may indicate market saturation or weak acquisition channels. Investigate whether acquisition spend is sufficient or whether the brand is overly dependent on its existing base.
- **Returning → Loyal conversion:** The 701 returning customers are the highest-leverage group. Targeted campaigns to push them past the 10-purchase threshold could grow the loyal segment.

### 3.8 Top Products per Category (Q8)

> *What are the top 3 most purchased products within each category?*

```sql
WITH item_counts AS (
    SELECT category, item_purchased, COUNT(customer_id) AS total_orders,
           ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY category ORDER BY COUNT(customer_id) DESC) AS item_rank
    FROM customer
    GROUP BY category, item_purchased
)
SELECT item_rank, category, item_purchased, total_orders
FROM item_counts
WHERE item_rank <= 3;
```

| Category | #1 Product (Orders) | #2 Product (Orders) | #3 Product (Orders) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Accessories** | Jewelry (171) | Sunglasses (161) | Belt (161) |
| **Clothing** | Pants (171) | Blouse (171) | Shirt (169) |
| **Footwear** | Sandals (160) | Shoes (150) | Sneakers (145) |
| **Outerwear** | Jacket (163) | Coat (161) | — |

**Category revenue breakdown:**

| Category | Transactions | Revenue ($) | Revenue Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clothing | 1,737 | 104,264 | 44.7% |
| Accessories | 1,240 | 74,200 | 31.8% |
| Footwear | 599 | 36,093 | 15.5% |
| Outerwear | 324 | 18,524 | 7.9% |

**Insight:** Clothing dominates with 44.7% of revenue and the highest transaction volume. Within categories, order counts are remarkably even (145–171 orders for top products), indicating a balanced product portfolio without over-reliance on any single SKU. Outerwear has only 2 qualifying products (Jacket and Coat), reflecting the naturally smaller category scope.

**Recommendation:** The balanced order distribution is a strength — no single product failure would cripple a category. However, Footwear and Outerwear are under-indexed relative to their category potential. Consider expanding the Footwear assortment or running category-specific promotions to grow its 15.5% revenue share.

### 3.9 Repeat Buyers & Subscription Propensity (Q9)

> *Are repeat buyers (>5 previous purchases) more likely to subscribe?*

```sql

```

| Subscription Status | Repeat Buyers (>5 purchases) | Share (%) |
|---|---|---|
| **Not Subscribed** | 2,518 | 72.4% |
| **Subscribed** | 958 | 27.6% |

**Insight:** Even among the most engaged customers (those with more than 5 prior purchases), **72.4% have not subscribed**. High engagement does not translate to subscription adoption. This is a conversion gap — the most behaviorally committed customers are not being captured by the subscription model.

**Recommendation:** This represents a significant untapped opportunity. The 2,518 unsubscribed repeat buyers are the **highest-probability conversion targets** for the subscription program. A targeted campaign (e.g., "You've shopped with us X times — here's what subscribers get") could materially grow subscription revenue.

## 6. Methodology & Technical Notes

### 6.1 Technology Pipeline

```
Raw CSV → Python/Pandas (EDA & Cleaning) → SQLite (database_customer.db) → SQL Analytics (10 queries) → Power BI (Dashboards)
```

### 6.2 SQL Techniques Employed

| Technique | Queries Used In | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `GROUP BY` + aggregations | Q1, Q3, Q4, Q5, Q9, Q10 | Segmented summarization |
| Scalar subquery | Q2 | Dynamic threshold calculation |
| `CASE WHEN` + conditional aggregation | Q6, Q7 | Ratio computation and rule-based segmentation |
| Common Table Expression (CTE) | Q7, Q8 | Multi-step logic decomposition |
| `ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY)` | Q8 | Ranked product analysis within categories |
| `ROUND()` | Q3, Q4, Q5, Q6 | Presentation-quality numeric formatting |
| `BETWEEN` | Q7 | Inclusive range-based classification |

### 6.3 Limitations

1. **No temporal dimension:** Transactions lack date/time stamps, preventing trend analysis, cohort analysis, and seasonality validation beyond the categorical `season` column.
2. **Sample composition bias:** The dataset is 68% male, which inflates male revenue contribution without reflecting true market potential.
3. **Single transaction granularity:** Each row represents one transaction, but Customer IDs are unique — preventing true repeat-purchase behavioral analysis within this dataset.
4. **No discount magnitude:** `discount_applied` is a binary Yes/No field. Without the actual discount percentage or dollar amount, we cannot measure margin impact.
